# StyleMeUp — Product Strategy: User Gaps, Longevity, Differentiation

Last updated: 2026-05-18 · grounded in a full read of the repo at commit `4799634`
(styleMeUp) and `53cc643` (the-edit). Not a vision doc — every claim below points
at something in the code or something missing from it.

---

## 0. What this product actually is (one paragraph)

A confidence engine in two rooms. The **Magazine** (void black) seduces with a
weekly editorial issue generated by `the-edit` for ~$1.20; the **Sanctuary**
(paper white) holds the user's own wardrobe. The bridge between the rooms is the
loop that matters: *editorial trend → "you have the base." → outfit from your own
closet → confidence.* Everything below is in service of making that loop tighter,
more personal, and harder to copy.

## 1. User-standpoint gaps (ranked by pain)

### 1.1 The promise–reality gap in Capture ⚠️ biggest
`screens/Capture/Capture.tsx` is a simulated camera — staged states, no real
photo, no ML recognition. The product's core premise ("the camera is the input")
is theatre right now. A user who captures three pieces gets pre-scripted labels.
**Fix path:** real camera via `expo-camera`, then a Gemini Flash vision call
(~$0.001/photo at current pricing) to label garment kind + color + material.
We already have the Gemini infra in the-edit; this is a weekend of wiring, not a
research project. Until then the app demos well and retains nobody.

### 1.2 The dead-end after onboarding
Onboarding is strong (cover → identity → starter pack → receipt → first
signature) and ends on a genuine high — Moment Yellow, generated outfit. Then…
nothing asks the user to come back. `lastDressingRoomDate` exists in the store
but no surface uses it. There is no daily reason to open the app and the weekly
issue badge is the only pull. **Fix path:** the daily look ritual (§2.1).

### 1.3 Audience routing stops at the starter pack
We shipped gender routing for starter-pack variants (`variantMatchesAudience`),
but **Discover is one-size**: the weekly issue from the-edit has a single
`audiencePersona` and the same trend cards regardless of rail. A man who chose
"man" at first fit still reads slip-dress styling angles in the issue. This
breaks the explicit promise of the identity screen. **Fix path:** the-edit
generates per-audience card variants (same trend, different styling angle +
imagery per rail) — pipeline change, ~2× imagine cost per issue, or audience-
split copy only (cheap) with shared imagery first.

### 1.4 Images load like a prototype
Remote photos use bare `Image` from react-native with no caching policy,
placeholders, or load-in motion. `expo-image` is already in package.json —
unused. DESIGN.md §11 specifies image load-in (400ms opacity + 1.02 scale, no
skeletons); nothing implements it. On cellular, Discover pops in raggedly.
**Fix path:** swap remote surfaces to `expo-image` (`cachePolicy="disk"`,
`transition={400}`) — one focused pass, big perceived-quality jump.

### 1.5 The Vanishing doesn't exist
The brand's signature transition (DESIGN.md §11, 1.3s, four phases) is specified
in 40 lines of detail and implemented nowhere. Skia + Reanimated are installed.
Every "try on" today is a hard cut. This is the single highest-leverage *feel*
investment — it's the moment users would screen-record and share.

### 1.6 Small frictions worth a polish pass
- Starter pack minimum (16) is communicated only after a failed continue.
- No haptics anywhere (§11 specifies five).
- Empty states for Closet/Looks exist in DESIGN.md §12 but only partially in code.
- No reduced-motion handling yet (a11y commitment in §13).

## 2. Longevity — retention mechanics that fit this brand

The brand bans streaks-with-flames and notification spam. Retention here has to
feel like *ritual*, not *obligation*. Four mechanics, in build order:

### 2.1 The daily look (the ritual) — build first
One outfit per morning, composed from the user's marked + captured pieces,
presented full-screen in Sanctuary register. Two actions: **wear.** / **next.**
(never thumbs — Tinder grammar is banned). `wear` is the strong positive signal
and feeds taste data; `next` re-rolls. This creates the daily open without a
single push notification — and when we *do* add one, it's editorial: *"today's
look is ready."* The store already has `persona`, `starterSelections`,
`capturedPieces`, `tasteNotes` — the raw material exists; the surface doesn't.

### 2.2 The weekly issue (appointment viewing) — already half-built
The-edit publishes weekly; the app badges unseen issues. Strengthen with: a
fixed publish day ("Thursdays, the issue drops"), an issue *archive* shelf
(volumes as collectible covers — magazine back-issues, not a feed), and a
60-second "issue trailer" motion cover. Appointment + collection psychology,
zero dark patterns.

### 2.3 Wear telemetry → cost-per-wear (the compounding asset)
Every `wear.` confirmation accumulates: pieces gain wear counts; the closet can
eventually show *"worn 14 times. earning its place."* This is data no competitor
can import and the user can't get anywhere else — the longer you use it, the
smarter your closet becomes, and leaving means abandoning your own history.
Quiet luxury version of a moat: **your wardrobe's memoir.**

### 2.4 Seasonal re-issues (resurrection mechanics)
The-edit's archive table already stores trend keywords + dates. Twice a year,
resurface: *"corduroy returned in vol. 18. it's back again."* Old issues become
living documents; the catalog compounds instead of expiring.

**Engagement metric recommendation (DESIGN.md §15.4 open question):** weekly
looks-worn, not daily streak. Streaks punish; looks-worn measures the actual
value delivered (got dressed, felt good). Home screen prioritizes today's look;
the metric stays invisible to the user.

## 3. Differentiation — feature standpoint

| Competitor shape | What they do | Why we're different |
|---|---|---|
| Pinterest / aggregators | infinite inspiration, zero ownership | our loop ends in *your closet*, not a saved pin |
| Zara/retailer apps | beautiful editorial → buy funnel | our editorial → *wear what you own* (anti-haul positioning) |
| "AI stylist" apps | chatbot + sparkle emoji + paywall | no chat UI, no AI badges; the AI is invisible, the taste is visible |
| Cladwell/Whering (closet apps) | utilitarian closet inventory | two-register brand + weekly editorial cadence they can't write |

**The defensible feature wedge, in one sentence:** *the only product where a
weekly fashion magazine is generated about trends, and every page ends with an
outfit assembled from clothes you already own.* The "you have the base." block
in Discover is the moat — invest there: deeper matching (garment kind + tone +
silhouette tags, not just ID overlap), and the empty state that says *"two
pieces short. here's the bridge."*

**Positioning sentence for the App Store era of AI fatigue:** every competitor
says "AI stylist." We never say AI (banned in DESIGN.md §4). We say *"the issue
drops Thursday."* That restraint **is** the differentiation — quiet luxury in a
category screaming sparkle emoji.

## 4. Differentiation — technical standpoint

### 4.1 The $1.20 content pipeline (cost moat)
A weekly editorial product whose marginal cost is $1.20/issue (steady-state,
archive-hit) with QA-gated brand voice. A human-written equivalent runs
$500–2,000/issue. This means we can ship **per-audience issue variants** (man /
woman / non-binary editions of the same trend) for under $4/week total — a
content depth no competitor matches at any sane burn. The pipeline's eval gate
(Vogue test, §5.5 headline patterns, grounded claims) is why the output doesn't
read like AI slop — that QA discipline is itself the moat, not the generation.

### 4.2 The brand bible as machine-enforceable spec
DESIGN.md is 21K tokens of bans and patterns that every generation pass is
checked against (cached prefix, 0.1× cost). Competitors prompt "make it
luxury"; we *diff against a constitution*. Replicating this requires writing the
constitution — months of taste work, not an API call.

### 4.3 Owned wear-graph (data moat, §2.3)
Capture + wear telemetry builds a dataset that doesn't exist anywhere: real
wardrobe contents × real wear frequency × editorial taste signals. That powers
recommendations no foundation model can cold-start.

### 4.4 Token-level cost discipline as a scaling story
Three-axis caps, salvage-on-cap, hybrid Pro/Flash routing, prompt caching —
unit economics are designed, not discovered. At 100K users the content cost is
still ~$5/week because issues are broadcast, not per-user; per-user AI spend
(daily look, capture labeling) stays under $0.05/user/month at Flash pricing.

### 4.5 Honest gaps to close (technical debt register)
1. **No analytics/instrumentation** — zero events tracked; we are flying blind
   on every funnel claim above. Add a thin event layer before personalization.
2. **No auth** — everything is device-local MMKV; no account = no cross-device,
   no recovery, no cohort analysis. Supabase Auth is sitting unused.
3. **Capture is simulated** (§1.1).
4. **The Vanishing unbuilt** (§1.5).
5. **Magazine issue payload unversioned** — `normalizeIssue` defends loosely;
   a schema version field would prevent silent breakage as the-edit evolves.
